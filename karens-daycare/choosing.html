Parent Guide

How to choose a daycare: questions to ask

The right daycare is the one where your child is safe and cared for, and where you feel at ease. This guide helps you work out what matters to your family and what to ask when you visit.

1. Start with what your family needs

  • Which days and hours do you need, including early drop-offs and late pick-ups?
  • How close is it to home or work?
  • What is your budget, and what is included in the fee?
  • When do you need a spot to start?

2. Understand the type of care

Home daycares and daycare centres run differently. A home daycare usually means a smaller group in a family setting. A centre usually means larger groups and more staff. Neither is better for every child. Ask each provider how they are set up, who is responsible for your child each day, and what happens if the caregiver is sick or away.

In Quebec, ask whether the provider is recognized through a home childcare coordinating office (bureau coordonnateur) or runs privately, and what that means for fees, subsidies, and oversight. A good provider will explain this clearly.

3. Safety and supervision

  • How many children are in the group, and how are they supervised throughout the day?
  • What are the arrival and pick-up procedures, and who is allowed to collect your child?
  • Is first aid training current, and what is the plan for an emergency?
  • Are outdoor areas and sleeping spaces safe and supervised?

4. Daily routine and learning

  • What does a typical day look like for your child's age?
  • How much time is spent on free play, quiet time, stories, and outdoors?
  • How are naps handled, and does the routine flex for individual needs?

5. Food and health

  • Are meals and snacks provided, or do you pack them?
  • How are allergies and dietary needs handled?
  • What is the illness policy, and when must a child stay home?
  • How are medications handled, if at all?

6. Communication

  • How will you hear about your child's day: daily notes, messages, photos, or a chat at pick-up?
  • How are concerns raised and resolved?
  • How much notice is given about closures or changes?

7. Guidance and behaviour

Ask how the caregiver responds when a child is upset, hits, or will not share. Look for calm, consistent, respectful answers that focus on teaching children what to do rather than punishing them.

8. Trust what you see on a visit

  • Do the children seem relaxed and engaged?
  • Does the caregiver get down to their level and respond warmly?
  • Is the space clean, safe, and set up for your child's age?
  • Do you feel comfortable asking questions, and are the answers clear?

Red flags to watch for

  • You are not welcome to visit, or are rushed through.
  • Vague answers about safety, group size, or who will care for your child.
  • No clear policies for illness, emergencies, or pick-up.
  • Children who look unhappy, ignored, or left unsupervised.
  • A space that looks unsafe, unclean, or crowded for the age group.
  • Pressure to commit or pay on the spot.
  • Your instincts say something is off.

This guide is general information to help you ask good questions. Always confirm requirements and your options directly with any provider you are considering.
