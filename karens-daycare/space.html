Parent Guide

What makes a good daycare space?

Children spend most of their day in the space around them. A good one keeps them safe, supports their age and stage, and invites them to play and learn.

It fits the age of the children

  • Furniture, toys, and activities match the children's ages and abilities.
  • Babies and toddlers have safe floor space to move, crawl, and explore.
  • Older children have room and materials for creative and pretend play.
  • Younger and older children are not forced to compete for the same space or toys.

It is safe

  • Clear sightlines so a caregiver can see every child.
  • Outlets covered, hazards out of reach, and stairs and exits secured.
  • Cleaning products and medications stored out of children's reach.
  • A clear plan for emergencies, with exits kept free.
  • Safe outdoor areas that are checked before children use them.

It is clean

  • Regular cleaning of surfaces, toys, and eating areas.
  • Handwashing built into the routine, especially before meals and after toileting.
  • A clean, separate place for diaper changes and a clear sleeping area for naps.

It supports play and learning

  • Different areas for different kinds of play: quiet reading, building, art, and active play.
  • Toys and materials children can reach themselves, rotated so they stay interesting.
  • Books, music, and open-ended materials that spark imagination.
  • A calm, uncluttered feel that is warm rather than overwhelming.

What to notice when you visit

  • Do the children look comfortable and busy?
  • Can you picture your child happily playing here?
  • Does everything feel cared for, from the toys to the corners?

This guide is general information about what to look for in any childcare space.
